Five-seed tuned baseline comparison, version 3

Verified all 40 baseline model results against saved CSV probabilities; validated URL/label ordering and partition fingerprints against main-model runs. Test set: 7,653 URLs. Positive class combines phishing, malware and defacement. Threshold 0.5.

Selection: modest predefined candidate grids, validation average precision, tuning seed 42; early stopping uses validation loss. Selected settings frozen across seeds. These are limited searches, not exhaustive optimization. Main-model architecture was fixed and was not tuned by this baseline search.

Mean +/- sample SD and Student-t intervals use five training seeds conditional on the fixed split and chosen configurations. Reusing the selected tuning model for seed 42 is disclosed; selection variability is not included. XGBoost uses full row/column sampling, and all five saved predictions for each variant are identical: zero seed SD does not mean zero generalization uncertainty.

Paired inference: baseline-minus-main accuracy averaged over seeds for each URL, then summed within PSL registrable-domain groups. Domain sign-flip test uses 20,000 random sign assignments, swapping the sign of the entire domain difference vector, and Holm correction across eight baseline-versus-main comparisons. This is conditional on trained models and assumes independent, exchangeable/symmetric domain-level differences under the null. 3,000 paired domain-bootstrap replicates resample complete domains, retain all URLs and the same five fitted seeds, and compute percentile intervals for mean-seed accuracy differences. These do not include retraining or tuning uncertainty. Do not pool seeds as independent test URLs. PSL snapshot/version may differ from original Windows environment; report the reconstructed domain count.

Exact McNemar per seed is supplied descriptively, but URL independence may fail within domains; domain-aware comparisons are the primary analysis. P-values are not probabilities that a model is better. No significance conclusion for F1 or AUC is made from accuracy tests.

Ablations are separately retuned on validation, so differences compare feature-availability pipelines, not a single fixed configuration's isolated feature effect. no_scheme drops is_https only: lexical URL lengths/slashes can still encode scheme information. no_scheme_no_ghana also removes is_trusted_gh_tld, brand_injected and brand_on_free_tld; it does not remove all possible geographic cues. Neural and numeric inputs remain different; same partitions alone do not make representations identical.
